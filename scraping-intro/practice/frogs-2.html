Frog directory

A practice directory of 30 real frog species, ten per page across three pages. The listing gives you names and links; each frog’s family, conservation status, range and photo are on its detail page.

Page 2 of 3 · 10 frogs per page · 30 frogs in total

  1. Moor frog

    Rana arvalis

    A slim, reddish-brown frog of wet meadows and bogs. Males turn blue for a few days during the breeding season.

  2. Northern leopard frog

    Lithobates pipiens

    A spotted green-brown frog with dark blotches and a pale ridge along each side. It is the state amphibian of Minnesota and Vermont.

  3. Pickerel frog

    Lithobates palustris

    A brown frog with dark, square-ish spots in rows along its back. It often lives near cold, clear water and wooded streams.

  4. Wood frog

    Lithobates sylvaticus

    A brown frog with a dark mask across its eyes. It can survive freezing and is the only North American frog found north of the Arctic Circle.

  5. American green tree frog

    Hyla cinerea

    A bright green tree frog with a pale stripe along its side. It lives in open forests near permanent water.

  6. Gray treefrog

    Dryophytes versicolor

    A small tree frog of eastern North America, usually found in trees near woodland ponds.

  7. Pacific tree frog

    Pseudacris regilla

    A small frog that can be green or brown and change colour over hours or weeks. It lives from sea level to high mountains.

  8. European tree frog

    Hyla arborea

    A small bright green tree frog with adhesive toe pads. It climbs trees and hedges and is found in much of Europe.

  9. Magnificent tree frog

    Pelodryas splendida

    A large green tree frog with white spots, first described in 1977. It has a small range along Australia's north-western coast.

  10. White-lipped tree frog

    Sandyrana infrafrenata

    The world's largest tree frog, with a pale lower lip that gives it its name. It lives in rainforests, cultivated areas and around houses in coastal areas.
